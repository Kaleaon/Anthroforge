## 1. The build poles — muscle × fat is a grid, and the genres name the cells

The single most useful structural finding: **bara's own taxonomy is a
two-axis grid** — muscle mass × fat layer — and the tradition names the
cells [artist, S — Wikipedia "Bara (genre)" body-type designations]:

| Term | Muscle | Fat layer | Nearest Western pole |
|---|---|---|---|
| *gatchiri* (ガッチリ) | high | low | bodybuilder |
| *gachimuchi* (ガチムチ, "muscle-chubby") | high | moderate | strongman / muscle-bear |
| *gachidebu* (ガチデブ, "muscle-fat") | high | high | strongman at max tissue, Sen-class |

Western strength culture independently lands on the same grid
[anatomy, S]: strongmen carry "immense muscle mass under a thick layer of
subcutaneous fat" at **15–25% body fat, up to ~30%**, vs bodybuilders at
**<10%, ~5% on contest day** (Eric Kim report); the coached "power look"
is defined as big neck/traps, thick back, large forearms, thick midsection,
big glutes/thighs, body fat under ~20% before "the fat hides the muscle"
(Spring Hill). **Construction consequence: a bara build is authored as TWO
vectors — a muscle vector and a tissue vector — and the pole is chosen
mostly by the tissue vector.** This is already AnthroForge doctrine on the
orc head (preset-orc-bara §2.3: tissue layer is the sub-preset switch);
this study extends it to bodies as a formal rule (§5, A1).

**Rugby is the real middle pole** [anatomy, S — Durham DXA study, elite
male rugby union]: forwards 190.1 ± 6.5 cm / 112.6 ± 15.3 kg at 23.2 ± 4.7%
total fat vs backs 180.3 cm / 89.4 kg at 17.4% — forwards exceed backs in
*every* regional mass, lean and fat alike, and the tight-five are the
heaviest/fattest subgroup. A "rugby build" preset = bodybuilder-class
muscle vector, tissue vector at ~⅔ of strongman, legs/arms athletic rather
than maximal. No current AnthroForge preset occupies this cell; §5 E-notes
flag it as the cheapest new pole to add.

## 2. Construction rules

### Frame and order of work

- **R1 — Skeleton and ribcage first, muscle second.** Every tutorial
  tradition converges: block the ribcage/box and pelvis, joints as circles,
  *then* hang muscle. "If the ribcage (the box) is wrong, the muscle will
  never look right" (Diffsense, citing Bridgman); the mannequin method's
  whole point is that the skeleton stays stable while muscle deforms
  (Drawing Codex); the swole-character reel likewise starts from ribcage
  structure, not added muscle (MRD Sensei — summary-grade source). [both]
- **R2 — Proportion before anatomy.** A wrong-sized primary form cannot be
  saved by detail (Drawing Codex; Skyrye). In slider terms: **frame
  sliders (shoulder incidence, sternum dimensions, torso width/length,
  leg size) are set before any muscle-group slider is touched.** [artist]
- **R3 — Muscles are grouped masses, never an inventory.** ~20 forearm
  muscles become two masses (thumb-side brachioradialis, pinky-side flexor
  group); the chest is two plates; the deltoid is one lemon/football cap.
  "Implied anatomy" — draw the big shapes and let the eye finish
  (Diffsense ×2; Egaco; Skyrye). Slider consequence: group sliders
  (TorsoMass, PecsSize) carry the read; micro-sliders (PecsCrease,
  TrapsValleys) are seasoning, applied last and lightly. [artist]

### The yoke — where "big" is actually read

- **R4 — The yoke is the primary size signal.** Traps + neck are "the
  first thing people see"; a jacked body under flat traps and a pencil
  neck "immediately looks way smaller, especially in clothes"
  ("How to REALLY Look Big" transcript). Coaching for aesthetics says the
  same thing in reverse: to look *less* bulky, avoid thickening neck and
  traps (Spring Hill). **In AnthroForge terms the traps/neck cluster is
  the highest-leverage slider group on any bara build — it is set first
  among muscle sliders and pinned near max at the strongman pole.**
  [both]
- **R5 — No lollipop: neck width ≥ jaw/cheek width.** "You never want your
  neck narrower than your cheeks… you want to look like a thumb"
  (transcript). AnimeOutline's version: muscular neck "almost as thick as
  the head," thickening as it descends. Floor metric M1 in §4. [both]
- **R6 — The traps are a FRONT-view structure, and they budget the
  clavicle.** The visible neck cone is trapezius wrapping from the back
  skull to the outer clavicle; the trap occupies roughly the **outer
  two-thirds of the clavicle run, the inner third is the true neck**
  (Bortekr reel — summary-grade; corroborated by Gvaat: trap "wraps around
  from the back to the front and inserts in the outer third of the
  clavicle bones… creates a cone-like structure around the neck"). A build
  whose front silhouette goes head → vertical neck sides → shoulders has
  no traps, whatever its back looks like. [artist]
- **R7 — The neck "cuts into" the shoulders.** Traps start *a little above
  the bottom of the neck* and slope down-out; the neck must appear to sink
  into the shoulder mass, not sit on top of it (AnimeOutline). This is the
  trap-slope metric M6 and visible-neck metric M7 in §4. [artist]
- **R8 — SCMs stay readable at every pole.** The sternocleidomastoid pair
  (jaw/ear base → sternum + inner clavicle) is the landmark that keeps a
  thick neck a *neck* and not a column of trap (Clip Studio; Gvaat;
  AnimeOutline). In 3D terms: the neck region's deformation must preserve
  a front V-groove — a QA note for the NeckMass slider in §5 A2, not a
  slider of its own. [artist]

### Torso masses

- **R9 — Delts are caps that overlap, in a fixed shingle order.** Deltoid
  (lemon-shaped) overlaps the collarbone end and the pec's outer edge; pec
  tucks *under* the deltoid; biceps under both; down the arm, delt >
  biceps > forearm (Diffsense; Egaco; Drawing Codex). A shoulder drawn/
  modelled as a ball on a joint reads as a doll's joint at any size.
  Slider consequence: ArmsDelts and PecsWidth must be raised *together* —
  delts without pec width (or vice versa) break the overlap read. [artist]
- **R10 — Pecs are two plates, upper share > lower share, and they cut
  into the shoulders.** Upper pec takes more visual space than lower
  (Clip Studio); chest mass should "somewhat cut into the inner ends of
  the shoulders" (AnimeOutline). PecsLower is a fullness slider, not the
  lead; PecsSize/PecsWidth lead. (Both orc sub-presets already obey this;
  Sen's PecsLower 70 is a tissue/age choice, not a mass choice.) [artist]
- **R11 — Lats are the taper.** From the front, lats read as the wing that
  connects mid-back to under the rear delts (Clip Studio); they are what
  makes width at the top *taper* rather than merely sit wide (MRD Sensei,
  summary-grade). TorsoBackSize/BackShape are front-silhouette sliders,
  not back-detail sliders. [artist]
- **R12 — The aesthetic pole protects the waist; the power pole spends
  it.** Aesthetic coaching: side delts, lats, upper arms, calves up; waist
  small; *avoid* the heavy compounds because they thicken waist, neck and
  traps (Spring Hill). This is why TorsoWaistSize ("Shrink Waist" — big
  end narrows, resolved in Titan batch 1) and the definition family are
  the bodybuilder pole's identity sliders, and why the strongman pole
  pins TorsoLower ("Enlarge Waist") instead. [anatomy]

### Arms — where artists and strongmen agree

- **R13 — Triceps make the arm; biceps make the pose.** Two arms of equal
  volume read completely differently depending on the triceps share; "the
  triceps — not the biceps — determine whether an arm looks truly massive"
  (MRD Sensei, summary-grade; consistent with anatomy: triceps ≈ ⅔ of
  upper-arm mass [anatomy]). HIMBO has no triceps slider — ArmsBicepsBack
  and ArmsSide are its carriers; on any bara vector they must track
  ArmsBiceps at ≥ ~0.7× its value or the arm reads posed, not big. [both]
- **R14 — Strength reads in the forearms.** Frazetta's Conan: "his strength
  wasn't even in his biceps; it was in his forearms" (Diffsense). The
  forearm is two masses with a *twist*: the thumb-side mass crosses over
  the top as the hand rotates (Diffsense; Apolline). Popeye is the limit
  case every artist knows. Forearm ratio metric M3 (§4) is the QA gate;
  Sen's pinned ArmsFore 100 "bear tell" (Titan batch 1) is this rule
  already in production. [both]
- **R15 — Antagonist pairing / volume constancy.** A contracted muscle
  shortens and thickens at constant volume (±1–2%, Sci Rep via Apolline);
  the antagonist must stretch and thin. This is a *pose-layer* rule, not a
  shape-slider rule — it belongs to the animation/expression system — but
  it forbids one shape-side cheat: never author a "flexed" morph that
  only adds volume on one side of a limb. [anatomy]

### Tissue, belly, posture — the strongman layer

- **R16 — Definition is a tissue dial, not a muscle dial.** The same
  muscle mass reads "bodybuilder" or "strongman" by the fat layer over it
  (15–25% vs <10% [S]) and by how much separation the tissue permits.
  AnthroForge already owns this dial: TorsoMassSmooth + Chubby + the
  definition family (TrapsValleys, BackDefinition, PecsCrease, VLine) run
  in opposite directions. **No bara preset should ever change its Muscle
  value to change poles — poles are tissue moves.** (Orc §3.1 vs §3.2
  currently violates this mildly — Muscle 55 vs 100; §5 B/C regularises.)
  [both]
- **R17 — The power belly is built, low, and inside the barrel.** It is
  (a) hypertrophied trunk musculature from bracing (rectus/obliques/
  transverse cross-sectional area grows under heavy compound loading,
  JSC 2018 via Workout Mag), (b) visceral + subcutaneous fat from surplus,
  (c) an anterior pelvic tilt component under load history pushing the
  lower abdomen out (Workout Mag). It is "round, firm, and protruding,
  often sitting low on the torso," and it *assists* bracing (Eric Kim).
  Construction consequences already half-built in belly v4: the belly's
  mass centroid sits LOW (v4 metric M1/M2′), the protrusion is continuous
  with flank/back mass (no silhouette step), and a posture shear (pelvic
  tilt) is a legitimate component of the shape, not a pose afterthought.
  What this study adds: the strongman belly must also NOT out-protrude
  the chest — Shaw's chest:waist is 1.28 with the chest still the widest
  ring [D] — the barrel is widest at the chest, the belly is its deepest
  *front* excursion. [anatomy]
- **R18 — Fat over muscle pushes out, it doesn't just cover.** Obliques
  trained under a fat layer push that layer outward; a wide waist at
  15–20% fat is usually muscle + fat stack, not fat alone (Israetel via
  WinterCycling). Slider consequence: strongman waist width comes from
  TorsoLower + LHandles + Belly fields *together with* Muscle held high —
  never from Chubby alone (Chubby-alone is the gachidebu-without-muscle
  failure, i.e. Fat Males read). [anatomy]
- **R19 — Legs carry the top or the build fails.** "Skip leg day" is the
  canonical amateur failure (Diffsense); strongman thighs are 70–85 cm
  class in powerlifters, 83 cm on Shaw [S]. LegsSize/LegsThigh pin at the
  same time as the yoke, not after it. The counterweight rule for anthro:
  on digitigrade builds the thigh mass must read through the digitigrade
  zigzag — the thigh is the one segment that keeps human-scale mass
  (furry-book corpus: thigh tubes "balloon out," lower leg abbreviated)
  [artist]. [both]

### Head, hands, scale

- **R20 — The head shrinks as the yoke grows — relatively, never
  absolutely.** Heroic standard is 8 heads tall, powerhouses 8.5–9 in
  superhero grammar [artist, S — Skyrye; Egaco gives 7.5–8 for muscular
  manga men]. Bara reads bigger-headed-mass by the opposite route: the
  head stays human while traps/neck/shoulder width grow around it, so the
  *ratio* does the work (head-width vs shoulder-breadth, metric M8; neck
  vs head, M1). AnthroForge already does this correctly (orc: head ×0.95
  relative at export, ~8 head-heights [E] in preset-orc-bara §3.0) — the
  rule is: **scale is applied to the body, the head is only ever nudged
  (×0.92–0.97), and M1/M8 are the gates.** Shrinking the head itself past
  that reads pinhead, the one failure bara shares with bad superhero art.
  [both]
- **R21 — Hands scale with the forearm, not the head.** The classic
  proportion "hand ≈ face length" is a lean-body rule; at the strongman
  pole the hand/forearm unit scales as one working tool (Shaw: size-16
  shoe and famously oversized hands at 203 cm [S, Rogue]; grip-sport
  culture measures forearm + hand as one stat line). Part-level rule for
  AnthroForge: hand part variants on bara builds scale with ArmsFore's
  tier (×1.0 / ×1.08 / ×1.15 [E]), never independently. [both]
- **R22 — Bara men are rugged, adult, and varied — finish is part of the
  build.** The genre's break from yaoi polish: beards, body hair, age
  spread, "vaguely rugged rather than handsome" faces (Wikipedia; Namu).
  For a *creator tool* this is a defaults rule: bara presets ship with
  body-hair coverage ON (fur system), brow/jaw emphasis available, and no
  default smoothing pass that would polish the build back to generic.
  [artist]

## 3. Hyper annex — exaggeration has rules too

Hyper-muscle (the genre beyond gachimuchi, and the register of artists
like the ones in today's bara/hyper roster) is not "bara but more." The
tutorial corpus is explicit that exaggeration is a *separate visual
language* with its own discipline [artist, S — Skyrye on Kirby-era
grammar; Diffsense cartoon-muscle essay]:

- **H1 — Scale the bellies, anchor the skeleton.** Exaggeration cheats at
  joints and foreshortening, never at the frame: the mannequin/skeleton
  stays stable while muscle masses deform around it (Drawing Codex;
  Apolline). In slider terms: **hyper tiers scale muscle-group sliders;
  frame sliders (Stature family) and joint-adjacent regions are NOT
  scaled.** A hyper build whose elbows, wrists, knees and ankles grew
  with the biceps reads as inflation, not muscle — the same failure the
  fat study found with BodyMass overuse (Titan batch 1 capped it at 30
  for exactly this reason). [both]
- **H2 — Four power zones carry the read.** Cartoon/hyper grammar
  concentrates on traps (literal slopes neck→shoulder), deltoids
  (cannonballs), serratus (a "second set of abs" on the ribs), and the
  neck (jaw merging toward collarbone) (Diffsense). Hyper budget goes to
  these first; everything else follows at a lower multiplier. [artist]
- **H3 — Forearm-led scaling.** The Popeye/Frazetta line (R14) steepens:
  forearm ratio M3 climbs with tier — ~0.80 at bara, 0.85–0.95 at hyper,
  ≥1.0 only at deliberate cartoon tier [E bands, anchored at the measured
  0.74–0.80 real band in §5]. Upper arms grow triceps-led (R13) so the
  forearm never reads as a transplant. [both]
- **H4 — Implied anatomy survives hyper; striation doesn't scale.** Even
  at cartoon size, the pros draw big shapes and leave fibres out —
  over-lined hyper reads "skinned," not strong (Diffsense). In 3D:
  definition sliders are NOT part of the hyper multiplier; hyper tissue
  stays smooth-to-moderate or the surface turns anatomical-chart. [artist]
- **H5 — The silhouette gate.** "Fill the character in solid black: can
  you still tell they're a powerhouse?" (Diffsense). Every hyper tier is
  QA'd as a flat silhouette before any surface work — for AnthroForge,
  a render-with-flat-material check at tier sign-off. [artist]
- **H6 — Head and hands are the last anchors to move.** Head scale holds
  at ×0.92–0.97 relative until the highest tier (R20), hands follow the
  forearm tier (R21). Hyper that shrinks the head early collapses into
  pinhead; hyper that grows hands independently collapses into glove.
  [both]

*Application:* AnthroForge hyper = a **tier multiplier record** applied
over a bara vector (zone multipliers: yoke/delts ×1.25, arms ×1.2
triceps-led, forearm extra +0.05 ratio per tier, legs ×1.15, frame ×1.0,
definition ×1.0), not a separate preset family. Two tiers (Hyper I,
Hyper II) suffice until a build proves otherwise. [E — authoring proposal
grounded in H1–H6, for builder calibration.]

## 4. Bear / otter annex — hair is a layer, not a build

Brief by design (the brief asked only where construction *differs*):

- **The builds are already in the grid.** Bear-culture taxonomy [artist,
  S — culture explainers]: bear = larger/stockier + hair; **muscle bear**
  = bear frame + defined muscle; cub = younger/smaller bear; **otter** =
  slimmer/leaner + hair; wolf = lean, athletic, muscular + hair. Mapped:
  bear = strongman tissue thesis (§1), muscle bear = bodybuilder muscle
  vector × bear tissue/hair, otter = lean-athletic vector (the rugby-back
  cell) — **the muscle grammar of §2 does not change at all.** [both]
- **What actually differs is three non-shape parameters:** (1) fur/hair
  coverage maps and density (the fur-prototype's region maps: chest,
  back, forearms, belly — bear high, otter ~0.6× and patchier), (2) beard/
  facial-hair part on, (3) tissue smoothing up half a step (hair softens
  every edge it covers, so the same definition values read lower — the
  muscle-bear variant in §6 compensates by raising definition, not
  muscle). [both]
- **Anthro-specific note:** on furred species the "hair" IS the coat —
  Sen's chest crescent precedent (Titan batch 1 §4.3: markings as
  fur-colour *zones*, never decals) is the correct implementation pattern
  for all of it. Bear/otter is a fur-prototype parameter set, not a
  preset family. [both]

## 5. Metric scorecard — how a build is graded

*Measure on the neutral-pose exported mesh. Circumferences = cross-section
loop perimeters at the named landmark; widths = front-view spans; depths =
side-view spans; angles from landmark points in front view. Bands are
targets for the QA gate, not beauty scores — a build outside its pole's
band is mis-built, whatever it looks like.*

**Reference bodies the bands are anchored on** [S unless noted]:
average US man (neck ≈40 cm, flexed arm 33–35, thigh 57, waist 102 —
NHANES via Nathan Tailors); Steve Reeves, classic BB (185 cm/97.5 kg;
neck = arm = calf 47 cm; chest 137, waist 76); rugby forwards (190 cm/
112.6 kg, 23.2% fat — Durham DXA); Eddie Hall (191 cm claimed, 164 kg
WSM-era; forearm 45, arm 59 armwrestling-era — Grip Gladiators); Brian
Shaw (203 cm billed, 191 kg; chest 152.4, arm 58.4 — Rogue; neck 53,
waist ~119, thigh 83, calf 55 — stats aggregators, weaker sourcing,
treated as [D]).

| # | Metric (measure) | Average | Bodybuilder | Rugby fwd | Strongman | Bara target | Hyper tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Neck width ÷ head width** (front span at jaw level ÷ bizygomatic span) | 0.82–0.88 [D] | 0.95–1.05 [D] | 1.00–1.10 [E] | 1.05–1.15 [D] | **1.05–1.20** | 1.15–1.30 [E] |

| M12 | **Heads tall** (total height ÷ head height, humanoid export) | 7.5 [S] | 7.5–8 [S] | 7.5–8 [E] | 7.5–8 [E] | 7.5–8.5 (see R20) | 8–9 [S, superhero grammar] |

Notes a builder must not skip:

- **M2's trap:** among *untrained* men the neck:arm ratio is >1 because
  arms are small; the ratio only discriminates among trained builds.
  Reeves' 1.00 equality (neck = arm = calf) is the classic-BB anchor;
  at the strongman extreme the arm outgrows the neck (Shaw 0.91) — M2 is
  a band, not a maximisation.
- **M3 is the most honest discriminator we have.** Training grows the
  upper arm faster than the forearm in bodybuilding (ratio falls to
  ~0.75), grip/strongman work holds it at ~0.76 with huge absolutes, and
  *artists* push it back up (0.80+) because forearm mass is the strength
  tell (R14). AnthroForge's bara band is deliberately set above the
  measured athlete band — flagged [E], that's an art-direction choice,
  not a measurement.
- **M5/M6/M7 are [E] calibration bands.** No tape-measure literature
  publishes trap angles or AP-depth ratios; the bands are set from the
  circumference anchors + photographic evidence and are exactly what the
  first builds should verify and tighten. Treat a miss as data.
- **M9 without M10 lies.** A 46 mass index at 20% fat (Shaw) and a 29 at
  26% fat (average) are opposite bodies. Never grade one without both.

## 6. APPLICATION MAP — what changes, where, by how much

*Executable without re-studying. "→" = change the value; "QA" = measure
after build, correct with the named sliders if outside the §5 band.*

### A. Global architecture (all bara work)

- **A1 — Two-vector authoring, formalised.** Every bara preset is
  documented as **MuscleVector** (frame + group mass sliders) ×
  **TissueVector** (Chubby, TorsoMassSmooth, definition family,
  TorsoWaistSize, belly family, PecsSaggy, RuffVolume). Changing poles =
  editing the TissueVector only (R16). Where existing presets let Muscle
  differ between poles of the same character, the builder unifies it per
  the lists below.
- **A2 — Build the `NeckMass` extension slider** (already flagged in
  preset-orc-bara §3.0; this study promotes it to required). Source
  field: HIMBO Weight block's neck-region verts + ArmsTrapsMeat field.
  Acceptance: at value 100 with traps pinned, M1 ≥ 1.05 and M7 ≤ 0.25 on
  the neutral orc head/torso join, **with the front SCM V-groove still
