# Fat reference — how heavy bodies are actually built

*AnthroForge first-proof, Part 1 of the v4 study (Nov 12, 2026). Commissioned by Joe's verdict on belly v3: "it still bulges weirdly, look at video of someone being heavier, or furry artist tutorials they make." Companion: `GRAVITY_V4.md` (the build this study drives).*

**Method note — what was actually seen vs inferred.** Read in full: the Keninism "How To Draw Fat Bodies" tutorial (text, via its Blogspot mirror of the original Tumblr post; its companion page by Mackenzie Lee on ArtStation carries the same text); a long-form drawing-fat essay ("Why Drawing Fat Still Trips Up Most Artists"); clinical pannus-grading sources (Igwe et al. 2000 grading as carried in the ASPS 2017 practice parameter, Kaiser Permanente's panniculectomy policy, and two surgical-practice summaries that quote the scale identically); an anthropometry study of obese adults (Sci Rep 2023, PMC10585005 — measured waist/hip circumferences by obesity class); and obesity-posture literature (Indian Journal of Pain review of obesity and spinal biomechanics; lower-crossed-syndrome clinical summaries). Video tutorials (Its AJ Art's belly-fat reels, Lucas Peinador's plus-size tutorial) were accessed through platform summaries/descriptions, **not** transcripts — YouTube was not transcribed for this study, and no rule below rests on a video alone; every video-sourced rule is corroborated by a written source. Numbers measured from photographs: none — where a metric's reference value comes from text rather than measurement, it is flagged [E].

## 1. The rule list

**R1 — Fat is a whole-silhouette system, never a local add-on.** [both]
The artist literature names two failure modes: the *"Anime Chubby"* (fat added only in attractive places on an otherwise thin body) and the *"Michelin Man"* (a thin body uniformly inflated, no distribution thought). v1–v3 committed a third sibling of the same error: a *correct* belly field on an untouched torso — lean chest, lean flanks, lean back, and a silhouette step where the belly mask fades out. Every source family agrees the unit of change is the whole body.

**R2 — Distribution follows muscle mass, in levels, and is uneven.** [artist, consistent with real]
Keninism's distribution chart: the more muscle an area carries, the more prone it is to store fat — abdomen first, then thighs, upper arms, chest, flanks, back. Fat sits **on top of** muscle and distributes in graded levels (next-to-none / moderate / most), *not* evenly. The real-pattern counterpart: adult males run **android** — abdominal-dominant — and at obesity class II–III the waist girth converges on the hip girth (waist:hip ratio ≈ 0.98–1.02, vs ≈ 0.85–0.90 lean) [S: PMC10585005].

**R3 — Fat pools at the lowest available point.** [both]
The water-balloon rule (Keninism; the drawing-fat essay): standing, tissue pools in the **lower abdomen, the outer thighs, and the underside of the upper arms**. A shape whose mass centres high is wrong before any detail is judged.

**R4 — The belly is rolls stacked downward, each lower roll at least the size of the one above — never one smooth circle.** [artist]
Its AJ Art's central correction, repeated across his tutorials: the beginner draws a single round belly, which reads as *pregnancy or bloating*; the fix is overlapping rolls with a gradual downward fall, the bottom roll the largest. (v1's dome is exactly the named mistake; v3's drain+drape mitigates but the underlying field is still one mass.)

**R5 — Creases form where tissue folds over itself and at compression points, and rolls are universal.** [both]
Named crease sites: the **under-belly (apron) crease**, the **waist crease** at the natural waist on the sides and back, the **groin crease**, the under-pec fold. Keninism: "everyone has rolls… they're just more prominent and more consistently present in fat people — pay close attention to where they are and how they're shaped." Clinically, the fold under the apron is where the pathology lives (intertrigo from grade 2 up) — it is a real anatomical feature, not a rendering garnish.

**R6 — The apron overlaps the pubic region, with a visible separation between belly and pubic mound; the mound itself gains fat and rounds in profile.** [both]
Keninism's hip/pubic tips: stomach fat *spills over onto the pubic region creating a separation between the two areas*, and the pubic mound fattens and rounds. Clinically this is the pannus scale itself [S: Igwe/ASPS]: **grade 1** the apron reaches the pubic hairline/mons; **grade 2** it covers the genitals; **grade 3** the upper thigh; **grade 4** mid-thigh; **grade 5** the knees. The same clinical sources note the panniculus "can include the mons pubis, upper abdomen, **flanks and back**" — distribution beyond the belly is in the medical definition, not just the art advice.

**R7 — The navel folds and buries as mass grows.** [artist]
Keninism: in fat people the bellybutton is *folded*; in thin people it is stretched out. (v3's dragged-down navel is consistent; a future detail pass can fold it.)

**R8 — The back carries real mass and folds that follow the ribs and lats.** [artist]
"People forget the back. Always." On a heavier person the back's folds become distinct masses following the rib line and latissimus dorsi; add the lower-back fat pad above the pelvis.

**R9 — Posture is part of the shape: anterior pelvic tilt + increased lumbar lordosis.** [real]
Obesity shifts the centre of gravity forward; the pelvis tilts anteriorly and the lumbar spine hyperextends to compensate (Indian J Pain review). Clinical tilt reference: ASIS–PSIS declination ≈ 5° in healthy men, > 15° is pathological tilt; obesity drives the angle up from neutral. Silhouette consequences: the belly tips forward-and-down, the lower back hollows, the buttocks project backward, the chest tips slightly back. A fat morph applied to a neutrally-posed lean skeleton misses this — the tutorials' version of the same rule is "respect the underlying structure (ribcage, pelvis) while adding volume" (Peinador).

**R10 — Touching tissue flattens and spreads (squish); thighs touch.** [artist]
Fat is soft and pliable: where masses meet (arm against torso, thigh against thigh) both flatten. Keninism is emphatic that a convincing fat character has **no thigh gap**. (Thighs are outside this build's torso scope; the rule is recorded for the legs slider family.)

**R11 — On a fat male chest, mass widens and sags; nipples angle down.** [artist, male-adapted]
The tutorials' chest rule (written for breasts, mechanically general): added chest mass *sags* and the nipples point more downward than straight out; an under-pec crease forms. Male visceral-dominant variant noted by the essay: a firm, tight-skinned protruding "beer belly" also exists in real men (visceral fat under a taut wall) — it is the *other* real pattern. Our target pattern is the subcutaneous pendant one Joe has been asking for since v2; the visceral variant is recorded as a future preset axis, not this build.

**R12 — Distribution is a person-level pattern, applied consistently.** [both]
Android vs gynoid patterning is a property of the *person*, not of each body part (clinical); the tutorials end the same way ("there isn't one single way for a person to be fat" — but each person's way is coherent). A slider system should therefore ship the distribution as one coupled field — which is what v4 does — not as independent part sliders that can contradict each other.

## 2. Silhouette metrics (the v4 scorecard)

All metrics are computable on a base+delta pair in the canonical frame (X right, Y forward, Z up). Reference values: [S] = sourced number, [E] = estimate/derived, flagged honestly.

|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Protrusion split**: share of front added-protrusion integral *below* the navel line | pendant pattern ≥ 55% below (R3/R4; the Fat Males sculpt's own mass centroid sits below its forward peak — crosscheck_fields.py hang index −2.58 cm, the only learned field below its peak) | [E] |
| M2 | **Apron lowest point** above the crotch/pubic landmark | pannus grades are landmark-based [S]: G1 = reaches pubic hairline, G2 = covers genitals, G3 = upper thigh. Our size class (navel anchor ≈ 12 cm, a moderate belly, *not* a clinical apron case) should land **between grade 0 and grade 1**: lowest belly point a few cm *above* the pubic line, roughly 0–8 cm above the crotch landmark | [S scale, E placement] |
| M3 | **Waist:hip girth ratio** (ellipse proxy from width/depth at the natural-waist band vs the hip band) | lean men 0.85–0.90; obese class II 1.02, class III 0.98 [S: PMC10585005]. v4 +1 target **≥ 0.95** (our belly is sub-BMI-40 class, so full convergence to 1.0 is not demanded) | [S] |

## 3. What this changes vs v3's parametric model

1. **The belly field stops being the slider.** v4's +1 = a *distribution field* over the whole torso (learned from the Fat Males full-torso morph and the HIMBO "Chonky" preset recipe — the two fattest artist-authored distributions we own), with the v3 belly (protrusion + pendant sag) crossfaded in where the belly mask is core. No region boundary carries a step (M4 is the gate).
2. **A waist crease is authored**, not hoped for: a shallow radial pinch at the natural waist on sides+back, depth solved against M7b.
3. **A posture shear is tested**: a small pelvis-anchored rotation field (anterior lobe at the pelvis/lumbar, slight posterior lobe at the chest, R9), kept only if it improves M1/M2 without costing folds.
4. **The scorecard is the reference's, not the model's**: v3 reported how well the field matched its own parameters; v4 reports pass/miss against M1–M8 above.
